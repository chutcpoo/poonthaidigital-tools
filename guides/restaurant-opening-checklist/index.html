Restaurant Opening Checklist: Get the Shift Ready Before the First Order

A good opening checklist answers one question: is the operation actually ready for service? It should verify the people, work areas, stock, equipment and unresolved issues that could slow the first rush.

Published September 20, 2026 · PoonthaiDigital Editorial Team

Quick answer

Build the opening routine around five controls: access & people, premises, stock, equipment & systems, and service priorities. Assign each task to a role, record exceptions, and finish with a manager readiness check.

Keep regulatory, food-safety and specialist maintenance records in the systems required for your location. This guide focuses on general operations readiness.

1. Start with access, staffing and the handoff from the previous shift

Before the team starts prep, confirm the people and information needed to operate the shift. The opening manager should know who is present, who is missing, whether access or keys are available, and what the previous shift left unresolved.

  • Confirm the opening manager or keyholder is present.
  • Review callouts, coverage changes and the first service period.
  • Read the previous shift handoff before assigning work.
  • Identify problems that must be handled before service rather than during the rush.

2. Walk the guest and work areas before calling them ready

A checklist should make “ready” observable. Instead of a vague task such as “check dining room,” define what the team needs to confirm: the area is usable, obvious cleaning or setup gaps are fixed, guest-facing materials are in place, and any exception is recorded.

Front of houseTables, service stations, restrooms, entrances, menus and guest-facing equipment are ready for the first customer.
Back of housePrep areas, storage access, tools and workstations needed for the first service period are ready for use.

3. Check stock by exception, not by counting everything every morning

The opening checklist is not a replacement for inventory control. Its job is to surface shortages that can affect today's service. Focus on critical ingredients, packaging, disposables and supplies that could stop or change a menu item.

If shortages appear repeatedly, move the recurring item into a proper reorder process. The free Inventory Reorder Point Calculator can help turn daily usage and lead time into a repeatable trigger.

4. Verify the equipment and systems needed for the first rush

Opening checks should focus on the systems the team depends on immediately: POS, printers, order screens, key production equipment, communication devices and other essential operational tools. Record problems with a clear workaround or escalation owner.

“Equipment checked” is too vague. A better checklist asks whether the specific equipment needed for today's service is available and whether an exception changes the operating plan.

5. End with a short manager readiness check

Staff completion and manager readiness are different things. The staff checklist records assigned work; the manager check asks whether the operation can actually start service. A final readiness pass should surface the few issues that matter most.

  • What is not ready?
  • What menu item or service process is affected?
  • Who owns the fix?
  • Does the team need a workaround before opening?
  • What must be handed to the next manager if it remains unresolved?

A simple opening checklist structure

TaskOne clear action or verification.
OwnerAssign by role where possible so the process survives roster changes.
StatusDone, issue found, or not applicable.
ExceptionOne factual sentence describing what is wrong.
Next actionWhat happens next if the issue is not resolved immediately?
Manager sign-offConfirm the operation is ready to begin service.

What not to put on the opening checklist

Do not turn one page into every system in the restaurant. Detailed maintenance history, formal incident records, full inventory counts and specialist safety logs belong in their own processes. The opening checklist should point to those systems when necessary instead of duplicating them.

How opening connects to closing and handoff

A strong opening routine starts the night before. The closing team should leave the workplace reset and record unresolved issues. The opening team reads that handoff, verifies readiness and creates today's priorities.
