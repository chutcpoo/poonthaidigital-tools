Restaurant Shift Handoff Checklist: What the Next Shift Actually Needs

A useful handoff is not a diary of the shift. It is a short record of what changed, what is unfinished and what the next team must know before service gets busy.

Published September 19, 2026 · PoonthaiDigital Editorial Team

Quick answer

Record five things: stock shortages, prep status, equipment or facility issues, customer/order follow-ups, and the next shift's top priorities. Add an owner and deadline only when follow-up is required.

Keep the handoff short enough to read in two minutes. If it takes ten minutes to understand, it is probably storing too much history instead of actionable information.

The five-part restaurant shift handoff

1. StockWhat is low, out, substituted or arriving late?
2. PrepWhat is finished, partly finished or still needed?
3. EquipmentWhat is broken, unreliable, switched off or waiting for service?
4. Customers & ordersWhat open issue, reservation, refund, special order or pickup needs follow-up?
5. Priority actionWhat are the first one to three things the next shift should do?

A simple handoff format

Use one line per issue. The fields below are enough for most independent restaurants and cafes:

  • Area: stock, prep, equipment, customer/order, staffing or other.
  • What changed: one factual sentence.
  • Impact: what happens if nobody acts.
  • Next action: the specific follow-up.
  • Owner: a role or person only when action is assigned.
  • Due: before open, before lunch, before close, or a specific time if necessary.

Example: bad handoff vs useful handoff

Too vague“Low on chicken. Machine acting weird. Busy night.”
Actionable“Chicken breast: 2 trays left; supplier delivery expected 10:30. Limit prep until delivery. Espresso grinder #2: dose drifting; use grinder #1 and leave #2 off until manager checks it.”

What should not live in the handoff?

Do not turn the handoff into a duplicate inventory system, maintenance history or incident archive. Long-term records belong in the system designed for them. The shift handoff should point to those records when needed, not reproduce them.

Connect the handoff to opening and closing routines

The closing team should leave the handoff after it finishes the final operational checks. The opening team should read it before assigning prep and service priorities. Use the free Restaurant Opening & Closing Checklist Starter to build that daily rhythm, then use the handoff for the exceptions that do not fit a repeated checklist.

When a spreadsheet becomes useful

A simple note works when the same small team works every day. A structured tracker becomes more useful when multiple shifts, managers or locations need history, ownership and consistent follow-up. The goal is not more fields; it is fewer missed actions.
