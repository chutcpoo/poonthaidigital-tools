What an opening and closing checklist is for

A restaurant opening and closing checklist turns recurring shift responsibilities into a visible routine. Instead of relying on memory, the team can confirm that the workplace is ready for service, note issues that need follow-up and create a cleaner handoff between shifts.

Five areas to cover in a starter checklist

Premises readinessWalk key areas and note anything that is not ready for service.
Stock readinessCheck essential items and flag urgent shortages.
Equipment readinessConfirm the equipment needed for the shift is available and ready.
Team prioritiesReview prep, service priorities and unresolved issues before the shift starts.

Opening checklist vs. closing checklist

An opening checklist focuses on getting the operation ready for the day. A closing checklist focuses on resetting, recording issues and leaving the next shift a clear starting point. The exact tasks should reflect your location, equipment, staffing model and applicable local requirements.

How to make a checklist easier to use

  • Group tasks by area or responsible role instead of one long mixed list.
  • Use short action language so staff can scan the list quickly.
  • Add a notes or issue field for exceptions that need follow-up.
  • Review the checklist when the menu, layout, equipment or staffing process changes.
  • Keep operational checklists separate from specialist safety, regulatory or maintenance procedures where appropriate.

Carry exceptions into the next shift

A repeated checklist handles normal work; exceptions still need a handoff. Use the Restaurant Shift Handoff Checklist guide for shortages, unfinished prep, equipment issues and follow-up actions that the next team needs to see.

Restaurant checklist FAQ

Should opening and closing tasks be on one checklist?

They can be, but separating them into clear sections usually makes the shift responsibility easier to understand. Larger teams may also separate tasks by front-of-house, back-of-house and manager roles.

How often should a restaurant checklist be reviewed?

Review it whenever the workflow changes and periodically enough to remove obsolete tasks or add recurring issues. The right review cycle depends on the operation.

Is this a food-safety checklist?

No. This page is a general operations starter and is not a substitute for local food-safety, employment, fire, security or other regulatory requirements.
