DAILY OPERATIONS

What belongs on a coffee shop opening checklist?

An opening checklist should make the shop ready for the first customer without relying on memory. Common categories include powering and checking equipment, preparing service areas, restocking frequently used items, confirming the register or point-of-sale setup, and reviewing any handoff notes from the previous shift.

The most useful checklist is specific enough that a team member knows what “done” means, but short enough to complete consistently. Equipment instructions, cleaning chemicals, food-storage requirements and cash procedures should always follow the shop’s approved operating procedures.

What belongs on a closing checklist?

Closing routines usually focus on cleaning, resetting, storing ingredients correctly, recording shortages or maintenance issues, completing cash or POS procedures, and leaving the next shift with a clear handoff. A checklist is especially useful for tasks that are easy to skip when the final hour is busy.

How to turn this starter into a repeatable system

  • Assign an owner to each task or section.
  • Separate daily tasks from weekly or periodic deep-clean items.
  • Write measurable completion criteria where possible.
  • Record exceptions such as low stock, equipment problems or unfinished tasks.
  • Review the checklist whenever equipment, menu or staffing procedures change.

Common checklist mistakes

  • Too vague: “clean bar” is harder to verify than a short list of defined surfaces or equipment steps.
  • Too long: a checklist that mixes every daily, weekly and monthly task becomes difficult to use during a normal shift.
  • No handoff: unresolved issues should move to the next shift instead of disappearing when a box is checked.
  • Using a generic list as a compliance document: food-safety and workplace requirements vary by location and business.

Coffee shop checklist FAQ

Should opening and closing tasks be in one list?

They can share one system, but separating them by shift usually makes the daily workflow easier to follow.

Can I use this as a food-safety checklist?

No. It is a general operations starter and does not replace local regulations, certified procedures or your business’s approved food-safety program.
