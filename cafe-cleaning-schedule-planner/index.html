How this cleaning schedule estimate works

The planner converts recurring tasks into a weekly equivalent. Daily tasks are multiplied by the number of open days. Weekly tasks are counted once. Monthly tasks are divided by about 4.33 weeks to estimate their weekly share. The total task count is then multiplied by your average minutes per task.

Weekly minutes ≈ (daily tasks × open days + weekly tasks + monthly tasks ÷ 4.33) × average minutes per task

This is useful for staffing and workload planning, not for deciding how often a specific surface or piece of equipment must be cleaned. Frequency should come from your actual operating procedures, equipment guidance and applicable regulations.

Worked example

A cafe with 8 daily tasks, 6 weekly tasks and 8 monthly tasks, open 7 days a week, has about 63.8 weekly task occurrences. If an average task takes 12 minutes, that is roughly 766 minutes, or about 12.8 staff-hours per week. With two people sharing the workload evenly, that is about 6.4 hours per person before allowing for setup, interruptions or unusually difficult jobs.

How to build a practical cleaning schedule

  • Separate tasks by frequency: every shift, daily, weekly and monthly.
  • Assign a role or owner so tasks do not become “everyone’s job” and therefore nobody’s job.
  • Put longer deep-clean tasks into lower-traffic periods where possible.
  • Track exceptions, maintenance problems and tasks that repeatedly run over time.
  • Review task duration estimates after staff have used the schedule for several weeks.

Common planning mistakes

  • Using one average for everything: a quick wipe-down and an equipment deep clean are not equivalent.
  • Ignoring setup time: gathering supplies, moving equipment and safe chemical handling can add time.
  • Overloading one closing shift: spreading appropriate work across the week can make execution more consistent.
  • Treating a template as a compliance standard: the correct procedure depends on your business and jurisdiction.

Cleaning schedule FAQ

Why divide monthly tasks by 4.33?

There are about 52 weeks in 12 months, or roughly 4.33 weeks per month. The conversion is an estimate for workload planning.

Does this planner tell me the required cleaning frequency?

No. It estimates workload from frequencies you already chose. Regulatory and food-safety requirements must come from authoritative local and business procedures.
